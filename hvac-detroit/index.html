When Your System Fails in Detroit

Call us about a problem and a real person answers, not a recording. Here's what we see most often when something breaks at home: warm air from the AC, a condenser that hums but won't start, a furnace that lights for a few seconds and quits, a breaker that trips every time the system kicks on, an evaporator coil that has frozen, a condensate drain backed up into the furnace cabinet, or a thermostat that has quit. Most of them trace back to a part we already carry: capacitors, contactors, igniters, flame sensors, universal control boards, and common fittings. You get the price before any work starts, with no surprises on the invoice.

Two common situations call for quick action from you before our technician gets there. The first is an iced-over air conditioner: turn the system off at the thermostat and set the fan to "on" so warm indoor air thaws the coil, because continuing to run it can overflow the drain pan and hurt the compressor. The second is a furnace that keeps cycling on and off or gives off a burning smell. Turn it off at the service switch and call us. Those few minutes can prevent a far bigger repair.

Keeping Your Detroit HVAC System Running Longer

A little routine care is the difference between a system that runs for years and one that quits in the middle of a heat wave. Our advice for homeowners is a short list: a new filter every one to three months, at least two feet of open space around the outdoor condenser, a spring rinse of the condenser coil, and a professional tune-up every spring and fall, so small issues get caught early.

How old is your system? Its age says a lot about what comes next. With regular service, most air conditioners make it to 12 to 15 years, furnaces 15 to 20, and heat pumps 10 to 15. If your system is beyond that range and also shows higher bills, uneven temperatures, or repeated repairs, it's usually due for a planned replacement, which is almost always cheaper and less stressful than a rushed emergency install in peak season.

Small problems rarely stay small for long. Take a weak capacitor: replacing it is a short, affordable repair, but left alone for a season, it can take the compressor with it, and suddenly a minor fix becomes a full system replacement. That's why we ask homeowners to keep our number in their phone and reach out at the first hint of trouble. Catching it early saves money and prevents a few miserable nights.
