Think about the spaces your central system has never handled well: a garage workshop that bakes by July, an addition the ductwork never reached, a bonus room that runs hot all summer, or an older Westland home built without ducts in the first place. Those are exactly the spaces a ductless mini split was made for. Nothing travels through leaky ductwork, because each wall-mounted head is fed by a line set that passes through a small hole in the wall. Inside the outdoor unit, an inverter compressor varies its speed to match the load, so the room holds within a fraction of a degree, quietly and on very little power. And when winter comes, it works as a true cold-climate heat pump.

Many disappointing mini splits were doomed on paper, not on the wall. The classic error is oversizing: a head too big for its room cools in a hurry, shuts off before it pulls out the humidity, and hunts all day. Westland HVAC sizes every zone to its real load instead. On multi-zone systems we also check that the outdoor unit's capacity makes sense across the heads it feeds and that every line set stays within the manufacturer's length and lift limits. Then comes placement. We walk the Westland home with you, weigh wall-mounted, ceiling-cassette, and floor-console options, and put each head where it throws air where you live and still looks right, with outdoor line sets dressed in line-hide rather than left bare.

Our installation process is held to the same standard as our biggest central systems. Nitrogen goes into the line set first to prove it holds pressure, then a vacuum pump pulls it down to a level confirmed with a micron gauge. Flares are torqued and leak-tested one by one, since that joint is where sloppy work shows up a season later. Drainage is set up with real fall or a quiet condensate pump, and the refrigerant charge is adjusted to match the line length. Finally, every zone is commissioned in both modes and you get a clear explanation of how the remote actually works. Why Professional Ductless Mini Split Installation Matters

Mini splits look simple hanging on the wall, and that illusion produces a steady stream of failed installs. The flare connections that join the line set are the system's weak point: under-torqued or badly formed flares weep refrigerant for months, and the first symptom is a head blowing room-temperature air a season later. Skipping the nitrogen pressure test and micron-verified vacuum leaves moisture in a circuit that inverter compressors handle badly. Line sets run past the manufacturer's length and lift tables quietly cost capacity, and a condensate drain without proper fall leaves a water stain the first humid week.

Sizing is even less forgiving with ductless than with central air. The smallest heads on the market are already more than many bedrooms need, so picking one size too big means short cycles, sticky humidity, and a room that never settles. That is why our experienced technicians size every zone on its real load, then back it up with careful install work: each flare torqued and leak-tested, the circuit pressure-tested and evacuated to a measured vacuum, charge adjusted for the line length, and every head commissioned in heating and cooling. Handling refrigerant is federally regulated, and the unit needs a correctly sized circuit and disconnect. Done this way, a mini split runs quiet and efficient for fifteen years with nothing to report.

Ductless Mini Split Installation Prevention Tips

A few simple habits now, like regular filter changes and seasonal tune-ups, can add years to your system's life. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Rinse or wash the indoor unit's filter screens monthly; they load up faster than furnace filters
  • Schedule professional cleaning of the indoor coil and blower wheel before buildup turns into growth
  • On humid summer days, confirm the drain line outside is dripping as it should
  • Clear snow, leaves, and clippings away from the outdoor unit year-round
  • Brief defrost cycles and a little steam in winter are normal, but a unit encased in ice needs service
  • Get a yearly professional checkup of charge, flare joints, and drainage while any fix is still small
  • Choose a setpoint and leave it alone, since inverters run best without constant on and off switching
