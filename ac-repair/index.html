An outside unit that hums while the fan stays still is the call we get most in July in Westland, MI, and the cause is usually a failed run capacitor. That inexpensive component starts the compressor and fan, fades for years without complaint, and then quits under peak load. It is far from the only suspect, though. Contactors pit and stop passing clean power, condenser fan bearings dry out, small refrigerant leaks drain away cooling capacity, and evaporator coils ice over when a clogged filter strangles airflow.

Warm air from the vents can mean a failed capacitor, a refrigerant leak, a frozen coil, or a duct problem, and each one comes with a very different price to fix. That is why Westland HVAC tests before touching anything. We meter capacitors against their microfarad rating, compare compressor and fan amp draws to the data plate, judge the refrigerant charge by superheat and subcooling instead of pressure alone, and inspect the coil, blower wheel, and filter whenever there is ice. Adding refrigerant without locating the leak only buys cold air until the charge leaks out again.

Warning Signs for AC Repair

Spotting the early warning signs gives you the chance to act before the situation spirals into something far more expensive. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • A hiss or gurgle from the lines, a common sign of leaking refrigerant
  • Ice on the refrigerant lines or a frozen indoor coil
  • Clicking or humming outside with no fan movement
  • A hot electrical smell or a tripped breaker whenever the air conditioner kicks on
  • Vents blowing air that is barely cooler than the room
  • Rapid on-off cycling that never brings the temperature down
  • Puddles near the air handler or water stains on the ceiling beneath it
  • Higher summer bills even though the house still feels warm and humid

Why Hire a Professional for AC Repair?

Start with the refrigerant. It lives in a sealed, pressurized loop, venting it is prohibited under federal rules, and correct service requires gauges, a vacuum pump, and a recovery machine rather than a store-bought recharge kit. Add capacitors that hold a dangerous charge with the power off and a condensing unit fed by 240 volts, and the risks add up quickly. Then there are the diagnostic traps: an iced coil often points to poor airflow rather than low charge, and topping off a system with an unrepaired leak lets moisture and acid slowly damage the compressor while your money leaks out with the refrigerant.

Professional repair gets the whole picture. Our experienced technicians meter electrical components instead of swapping them, weigh charge decisions against measured superheat and subcooling, find leaks with detectors and dye rather than assumptions, and check the filter, blower, and duct static pressure whenever airflow is suspect. That is the difference between a repair that holds through the season and a parts-cannon fix that calls you back in three weeks. And when the system genuinely is not worth saving, we tell you that plainly too, with numbers instead of pressure.

Maintenance Tips for AC Repair

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly heating and cooling problems down the road. Follow these tips from our experienced technicians:

  • Never enclose the condenser; it needs open space on every side to shed heat
  • Leave vents open in every room, since closing them strains the blower
  • Listen for new buzzing or clicking at startup, the classic sound of a dying capacitor
  • Clear the condensate drain when you first turn on the AC each year
  • Swap filters monthly while the AC is running hard to keep the evaporator coil from icing
  • Keep shrubs two feet from the outdoor unit and gently rinse its coil each spring
  • Have the AC serviced every spring, ahead of the first real heat
