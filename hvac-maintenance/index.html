Warning Signs for HVAC Maintenance

Spotting the early warning signs gives you the chance to act before the situation spirals into something far more expensive. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Filters you cannot remember changing, or a musty smell when the blower starts
  • Nobody has opened up the system in over a year
  • New noises when the equipment starts up
  • Energy bills creeping up without any change in your habits
  • You simply have no idea what condition your equipment is actually in
  • Recently installed equipment whose manufacturer expects maintenance records
  • A surprise breakdown during the last hot or cold spell
  • Longer run times to get to the thermostat setting

Why Hire a Professional for HVAC Maintenance?

Homeowners can take care of a short list: changing filters on time, keeping the outdoor coil clear, leaving registers open, and checking the drain. The rest takes instruments, because the problems that leave you stranded give no visible warning. A capacitor that has lost 20 percent of its rating looks the same as a new one. Low charge shows up only in superheat and subcooling readings. Cracked heat exchangers, weak flame signals, marginal pressure switches, and amp draws closing in on the breaker's limit are all invisible until measured, and all of them cost less to fix the season before they fail.

On fuel-burning equipment, maintenance is also about safety. A failing burner, flame proving circuit, vent, or draft problem can put combustion products where they should never be, which is why those items get instrumented verification every year. Westland HVAC makes those safety checks the core of each fall visit, never an upsell. How to Prevent HVAC Maintenance Problems

Smart maintenance is almost always cheaper and easier than an early replacement. Follow these tips from our experienced technicians:

  • Trim plants back from the outdoor unit and give the coil a gentle rinse every spring
  • Test carbon monoxide detectors monthly if any of your equipment burns fuel
  • Get your tune-ups on the calendar during mild weather, well before the rush
  • Change the filter every one to three months; it is the single highest-value habit there is
  • Pay attention to new noises or longer cycles and mention them at your next visit
  • Flush the condensate line at the start of cooling season before algae blooms in it
  • Leave every supply and return register open, including those in unused rooms
